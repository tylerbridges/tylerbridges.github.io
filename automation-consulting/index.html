THE BEGINNING

A request arrives. The owner starts from scratch.

Every inquiry means reading the message, collecting the details, deciding what to ask, and writing a response.

CUSTOMER MESSAGE

Hi! We’re looking for help cleaning our house before family visits next month. It’s about 1,800 square feet, three bedrooms and two bathrooms. We’d like the kitchen and bathrooms cleaned thoroughly, plus the usual floors and dusting. We have a dog. Ideally a Friday morning. What would it cost? — Alex

02

THE PROPOSED SOLUTION

A reusable AI instruction for that one task.

The first version can be as simple as an instruction the owner uses with an AI tool and a customer message. We tailor the response style and business rules, then try representative inquiries.

WHAT THE TOOL IS ASKED TO DO

Summarize this cleaning inquiry using only the information supplied. List missing details needed to prepare a quote. Draft a brief, friendly response asking for those details. Do not invent prices, availability, contract terms, or guarantees. Flag decisions for the owner to confirm.

In a pilot, the owner provides approved service details and policies so the instructions fit their business.

THE PROOF OF CONCEPT

Here’s what a useful result would look like.

The same inquiry becomes a summary, a list of open questions, and a draft the owner can adapt.

SUMMARY

  • One-time house cleaning before a family visit.
  • 1,800 sq ft · 3 bedrooms · 2 bathrooms.
  • Kitchen, bathrooms, floors and dusting.
  • Dog in the home · Friday morning preferred.

Still needs checking

  • Exact date and service area.
  • Whether extras such as the oven or refrigerator are needed.

HOW IT HELPS IN PRACTICE

The owner reviews a starting point and takes the next step.

From drafting to reviewing

The owner checks the summary against the original message, confirms any business details, edits the draft, and sends it through their usual email tool. Missing information is requested before a quote is prepared.

What we test before calling it a solution

Try several realistic requests. Check whether details stay accurate, important questions are caught, and replies need fewer edits. Compare the time spent with the owner’s usual process.

This illustrates a proposed workflow. Actual usefulness and time savings need to be tested with the business owner.
